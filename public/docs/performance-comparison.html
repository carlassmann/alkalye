What these results say

The rewrite's small bundle does not guarantee faster document opening. Its 32 KB switching time is about 41 times the original's median, and its edit response is about nine times slower. Those regressions are much larger than the variation between samples.

For short notes, the rewrite reloads faster, renders preview faster and propagates online edits faster. Both implementations saved edits offline, reopened them after reload, and delivered them to a second browser after reconnect. Reconnect results have only three samples each.

How timing works

A probe starts its clock inside the first trusted pointer, key or input event. It waits for the expected source or DOM state, then records the next animation frame. Reload timing starts at navigation and ends when the correct source exists in a visible editor, plus one animation frame. Neither metric proves that every pixel has appeared or an entrance animation has finished.

Peer sync uses two independent browser processes restored to the same disposable account. The producer records performance.timeOrigin + performance.now() at trusted input. The peer records the same epoch clock two animation frames after its source contains the unique marker. These timings include each application's save scheduling and local relay work.

Offline runs disable networking, append a unique marker, wait for local save acknowledgement, confirm the peer does not have the marker, and reload while still offline. Reconnect timing starts at the producer's online event and ends at the peer's paint opportunity.

Reload, find, preview, switching and online sync have nine samples each. Insertions have twelve. Offline reload and reconnect have three. Medians use the arithmetic midpoint for an even count. The raw JSON includes every sample. Its nearest-rank p95 is the maximum for these small groups, so no reliable tail-latency claim is made.

Rewrite b4556e83d2db31302f7942ffe826d73fc3bbfd24. Local production build, served by its native Bun server with an isolated SQLite database and asset directory. Both PWAs had active service workers and warm caches.

Fixtures started at 2, 32 and 128 KiB. Typing grows them during the run. Initial library sizes differed by one blank document. Tests ran the original before the rewrite within each phase. Local accounts and data were isolated from the app you use.

Excluded attempts and limits

Discarded original runs made before its service worker installed. Discarded an undo shortcut run that stalled browser automation. Repeated the original offline reload with an inline cached probe after the external probe failed to load. Repeated the rewrite find journey after correcting sidebar setup.

  • Timing measures paint opportunity, not pixel presentation or animation completion
  • Shared host had other Chrome processes consuming CPU
  • No WAN, phone hardware, network throttling, concurrent-edit conflict load, whiteboard, slideshow, cold-cache installation, long-poll-only or asset-load benchmarks
  • Save acknowledgements differ across implementations and are not compared as disk durability
  • Different initial library sizes: original welcome + 3 fixtures; rewrite welcome + blank + 3 fixtures
  • No confidence intervals or randomized version order; narrow timing differences need a controlled rerun
