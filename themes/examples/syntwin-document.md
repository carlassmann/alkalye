# Preparing a useful conversation

A Syntwin gives people a focused way to talk with a curated perspective. Start with a purpose that is useful to a real person, then make the knowledge behind it easy to inspect and improve.

## Define the twin’s purpose

Write one sentence that describes who the twin is for and the kind of conversation it should support. Keep the boundary clear enough that a visitor knows what to ask.

- Name the audience and the question they are trying to answer.
- Choose a voice that fits the subject and the people sharing it.
- State what the twin should hand back when it cannot help.

> A useful twin makes its perspective clear before it makes a claim.

## Curate knowledge with care

Add the documents, notes, and references a careful human would reach for. Prefer a small set of current, legible material over a large unreviewed collection.

| Check     | Prompt                                      | Result            |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Relevance | Does this help the intended conversation?   | Keep or remove    |
| Context   | Can a reader understand where it came from? | Add a note        |
| Currency  | Is it still a fair representation?          | Review or replace |

```text
purpose → knowledge → conversation → feedback → review
```

## Test before sharing

Ask the twin a few ordinary questions, then a few difficult ones. Look for unsupported certainty, missing context, and answers that do not sound like the intended perspective.

## Review what people learn

Share the twin with a small group first. Collect the questions it helped with, the gaps it revealed, and the material that needs attention. Use that feedback to keep the next conversation more useful than the last.
